# Video Script

Working draft of the demo video. About 2:30. The voiceover lines are placeholders to rewrite in your own words.

**Style:** doodle stick figures (round heads, dot eyes, noodle limbs, mitten hands). The rider is drawn in clean, bolder ink. The world around him is loose pencil under watercolour. Reference frames: the storyboard artifact (shots 17–21 come straight from it).

**Colour carries the story:** the bad afternoon is cool and washed out, and his coral hoodie is grey. After the rewind everything is warm, and the coral is back.

**The app name is never said until the last two words.** "On the way" closes the voiceover and becomes the title card.

## Voiceover takes

Same 25 shots for both; only the narration changes. Numbers in brackets are shots.

### Take A: calm storyteller

> **[1–3]** Picture a student who travels about eighty kilometres a day, just to get to college and back. *(beat)* The bus is his only way there.
>
> **[4–5]** The timetable says his bus home leaves at two-thirty. So when class ends, he doesn't stop. Not for friends, not for chai. He runs.
>
> **[6]** There's no live tracking. Just a timetable, and the timetable is a guess.
>
> **[7]** *(silence, only the clock)*
>
> **[8]** Twenty-nine minutes at a roadside. And tomorrow, the same again.
>
> **[9–11]** And he isn't the only one. Thousands of people ride UPSRTC buses every day, and every one of them is guessing. They don't need another map. They need to know one thing: *when do I leave?*
>
> **[12–13]** Here's the thing. The conductor already cuts a ticket at every stop. Same machine. Same routine. Nothing new to learn.
>
> **[14]** But every ticket says exactly where that bus is, right now. And that goes straight to the people waiting for it.
>
> **[15]** Every trip teaches the timetable, so tomorrow's guess is better than today's.
>
> **[16–19]** Same afternoon. One difference. He stays. *(beat)* His phone buzzes: the bus is twenty-seven minutes late. Plenty of time.
>
> **[20]** And when it's time, it tells him: *leave.*
>
> **[21]** Two minutes at the stop, instead of twenty-nine.
>
> **[22]** No direct bus? It plans the change for you. Want alerts? One tap. No account.
>
> **[23]** One student, eighty kilometres a day. Thousands like him.
>
> **[24–25]** So finish your chai. Stay with your friends. We'll let you know when your bus is… *on the way.*

### Take B: dry and punchy

> **[1–3]** This is a student. Every day, he travels eighty kilometres to get to college and back. *(beat)* By bus. Only by bus.
>
> **[4–5]** The timetable says 2:30. So the moment class ends, he sprints. Friends? Later. Chai? Later.
>
> **[6]** Live tracking? Doesn't exist. What he has is a timetable. Which is, let's be honest, a suggestion.
>
> **[7]** *(silence, only the clock)*
>
> **[8]** 2:59. Twenty-nine minutes of standing in the sun. Repeat tomorrow.
>
> **[9–11]** Now multiply him by thousands. That's everyone who rides UPSRTC on any given day, all guessing. Nobody's asking for a fancy map. They're asking: *when do I leave?*
>
> **[12–13]** Meanwhile, the conductor is doing what he always does: cutting tickets, stop after stop. We didn't change a thing for him.
>
> **[14]** But each one of those tickets says where the bus actually is. So we send it straight to the people waiting.
>
> **[15]** And every trip makes the timetable a little less wrong.
>
> **[16–19]** Rewind. Same afternoon. This time he sits down. *(beat)* Buzz: twenty-seven minutes late. Great. Chai first.
>
> **[20]** Then, right on cue: *leave.*
>
> **[21]** Two minutes at the stop. Not twenty-nine.
>
> **[22]** Need to change buses? Planned. Want alerts? One tap, no sign-up.
>
> **[23]** One student. Thousands like him.
>
> **[24–25]** So stay. Finish your chai. We'll tell you when your bus is… *on the way.*

## Production notes

- **Build:** Remotion, in its own `video/` folder outside the app workspace. Shots are React components drawing the same SVG doodles; camera moves are scale and translate on a wrapping group.
- **Voice:** record after the script is final. Shot lengths get timed to the recording, not the other way round.
- **Cuts if it runs long:** shorten the timelapse (7), then drop the montage (22).
